\section{Evaluation and Model Results}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Evaluation covers principle classification, text-vagueness screening, comparison with historical human ratings, and verification of the service connection. Each result identifies the text group or company cohort and the reference used for comparison. Classifier and mapping results use the recovered seed-99 checkpoint. Company-score applications are presented separately in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\subsection{Evaluation measures and uncertainty}

Top-1 agreement is the proportion of text segments whose highest-ranked principle matches the recorded weak label. Top-2 agreement counts a match when the label appears among the two highest-ranked principles. Precision describes how often a predicted principle matches the reference, while recall describes how much of a reference category is recovered. For principle $k$, F1 combines precision $P_k$ and recall $R_k$ through the harmonic mean:
\begin{equation}
F1_k=\frac{2P_kR_k}{P_k+R_k},\qquad
\operatorname{MacroF1}=\frac{1}{9}\sum_{k=1}^{9}F1_k.
\label{eq:f1}
\end{equation}
Macro-F1 averages the nine principle-level F1 values with equal weight, preventing the largest class from dominating the measure. Support is the number of reference text segments for a principle. All classification measures assess agreement with report-derived labels rather than independent semantic judgements.

Classification and mapping intervals use 2,000 company-cluster bootstrap samples with seed 4206. Companies are drawn with replacement, retaining all text segments from each selected CIN, and paired comparisons use the same draw for both methods. The 20-company score comparison uses 10,000 bootstrap samples of company-score pairs with seed 42. The resulting 95\% confidence intervals describe sampling variation within the available data. Detector confidence intervals were not calculated. The supervised split has no shared companies, but the earlier masked-language-model builder reads across the company database, and the exact adaptation-corpus membership of the selected encoder is unverified. Company-disjoint claims therefore concern supervised fine-tuning. The reported recovery results re-evaluate the established test group rather than a newly collected cohort.

\subsection{Classifier performance and training history}
\label{sec:training_history}

The recovered classifier matches 5,767 of 6,201 test labels, giving 93.00\% Top-1 agreement \mbox{(95\% CI: 92.04--93.89\%)}. The reference label appears among the two highest predictions for 6,057 text segments, giving 97.68\% Top-2 agreement \mbox{(95\% CI: 97.10--98.21\%)}. Macro-F1 is 0.9158 \mbox{(95\% CI: 0.9036--0.9277)}. Although the supervised splits share no CINs, 50 test text segments exactly match training wording from other companies, accounting for 0.81\% of the test group.

Figure~\ref{fig:training} shows the five recorded epochs for seed 99. Validation macro-F1 increases from 0.9048 at epoch 1 to 0.9194 at epoch 4, followed by 0.9189 at epoch 5. The selection criterion therefore retains epoch 4. Validation loss rises over the same period because loss also reflects prediction confidence, whereas macro-F1 measures the balance of category decisions. Table~\ref{tab:per_principle} and Figure~\ref{fig:per_principle} report performance across all nine principles. P7 has 69 test text segments, compared with 1,715 for P3, making class support important when interpreting the individual scores.

\subsection{Generalisation to excluded reporting concepts}
\label{sec:concept_diagnostics}

Company separation tests new supervised company groups, while concept exclusion tests reporting questions omitted from labelled training. Excluding 17 Section C concepts produces an evaluation group of 1,190 text segments. On this same group, the full-concept model achieves 97.98\% Top-1 agreement and the excluded-concept model achieves 87.31\%. The paired difference is $-10.67$ percentage points \mbox{(95\% CI: $-12.36$ to $-8.96$)}. Both checkpoints passed strict parameter-name recovery. The full-concept model uses seed 99, whereas the excluded-concept model uses seed 42 and fewer training text segments; the observed difference therefore reflects these conditions together.

Across three excluded-concept runs, mean Top-1 agreement is 81.64\%, with a standard deviation of 4.92 percentage points. Mean Top-2 agreement is 95.34\% (standard deviation 1.31 percentage points), and mean macro-F1 is 0.6752 (standard deviation 0.0170). Figure~\ref{fig:concept} shows the individual results and evaluation sizes. These standard deviations describe variation across the three runs, not confidence intervals. All runs reuse the original test-company group, and concept exclusion applies to labelled training; the adaptation-corpus condition described above still applies.

\subsection{Fixed text-vagueness detector}

Table~\ref{tab:detector} reports two separate label groups under fixed detector settings. The development group contains 80 text segments, with 37 labelled vague. The detector raises 41 flags, of which 29 match the reference. Precision is therefore $29/41=0.7073$, and recall is $29/37=0.7838$. Sixty decisions agree with the reference, giving accuracy of 0.7500 and F1 of 0.7436. Blinding of the earlier development labels is not documented, so this result is reported as a development comparison.

The second group contains 70 AI-annotated text segments. The detector raises 35 flags, with 21 matching the 26 AI-vague labels, giving F1 of 0.6885. Figure~\ref{fig:detector} compares precision, recall, and F1 across the two groups. The AI comparison is exploratory rather than independent human validation, and the groups are not pooled because the text and reference sources differ.

\subsection{Transparency Score and historical human ratings}

Across 20 historical company ratings, Pearson correlation between automated and human scores is $r=0.6314$ \mbox{(95\% CI: 0.2683--0.8519; $p=0.0028$)}. Spearman rank correlation is $\rho=0.6285$ \mbox{(95\% CI: 0.2321--0.8540)}. Mean absolute error is 12.16 points, and root mean squared error is 15.19 points. The squared correlation is $r^2=0.3986$, describing association without a separately fitted predictive regression. These measures report both how the scores vary together and the size of the score differences.

All 20 ratings come from one documented source, and rater blinding is not recorded. The original Zensar packet contains a URL-related extraction issue, while Geojit's business model differs from manufacturing-oriented reporting items. Both companies remain in the reported cohort. The comparison retains the original ratings and fixed score formula; synthetic ratings and AI assessments are separate records.

A principle-mapping request for 360 ONE WAM LIMITED on 19 October 2026 returned the recovered seed-99 method identifier and checkpoint path.
