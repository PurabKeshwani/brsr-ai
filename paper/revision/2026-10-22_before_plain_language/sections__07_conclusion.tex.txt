\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Large sustainability-disclosure collections require more than a search for convincing or cautious language. An analyst also needs the relevant filed value, reporting context, and a clear explanation of the resulting company score. GRASP connects these needs through structured BRSR storage, taxonomy-derived labels, principle classification, language screening, filing-consistency review, and a decomposed Transparency Score. The contribution is a connected and traceable workflow: the same filing record anchors narrative and numerical review, the label source remains visible during evaluation, and every score component has an explicit calculation. These features support review prioritisation without equating a risk signal with verified misconduct.

The recovered classifier achieves 93.00\% Top-1 agreement with weak labels (95\% CI: 92.04--93.89\%) and macro-F1 of 0.9158 on 6,201 segments from 175 companies excluded from supervised fine-tuning. Agreement varies by source: 63.35\% for explicit principle-axis narratives, 95.15\% for concept-mapped narratives, and 90.72\% for principle notes. Lower agreement on excluded concepts further limits a claim of broad semantic generalisation. The frozen text-vagueness component reaches F1 of 0.7436 on 80 development examples; complete numerical-verification accuracy remains unestablished. The fixed score has Pearson correlation $r=0.6314$ with 20 historical human ratings (95\% CI: 0.2683--0.8519), with annotation blinding unconfirmed. Each result therefore retains a distinct evaluation population and evidence limit.

The saved score snapshot covers 1,164 companies across 72 recorded sectors and supports four levels of reporting: one worked company, comparisons within a selected sector, the complete financial-sector group, and an all-sector summary. Financial-company scores span 29.83--67.56. Among the 47 sectors containing at least five companies, Wearing Apparel has the lowest descriptive mean, 42.33, while Warehousing and Transport Support has the highest, 54.55. The eight available paired-year companies show a mean increase of 5.23 points, from 45.17 to 50.40, with six increases and two decreases. This small group illustrates how component changes explain a score trajectory; the observation does not establish market-wide progress or improved sustainability outcomes. Together, the company, sector, and year comparisons make the outputs interpretable while preserving the distinction between disclosure measurement and independent validation.

Future work should strengthen the evidence behind each analytical output. Independent semantic labels and broader blind human ratings would support more reliable assessment of principle classification, language flags, and score interpretation. Encoder adaptation should be restricted to the declared training population, followed by evaluation on a newly collected company cohort. Numerical review should match metric meaning, units, dimensions, reporting boundaries, and valid zero values before assigning a consistency signal. Data coverage could expand to unstructured annexures and charts, while operational descriptions could improve sector assignment and indicator applicability. Additional annual filings could support a balanced multi-year panel with a fixed scoring version and recorded filing identifiers. Finally, compatibility with international sustainability frameworks could be examined through explicit taxonomy and reporting-context mappings, with separate validation for every new setting.
