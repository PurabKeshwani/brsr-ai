\section{Evaluation Design and Audited Model Results}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The evaluation separates four evidence types: agreement with taxonomy-derived labels, comparison with historical development labels, association with historical human scores, and descriptive company outputs. The authoritative values come from the consolidated audit and the recovered-result JSON files. Earlier manuscript values, unverified ensemble results, and synthetic-rating correlations are excluded from the headline results.

\subsection{Populations, metrics, and uncertainty}

For principle classification, Top-1 agreement is the fraction of segments whose highest-ranked principle matches the reference label. Top-2 agreement is the fraction whose reference label appears among the two highest-ranked principles. Top-2 offers a useful candidate list for an analyst, but a larger Top-2 value does not represent an increase in single-answer accuracy.

Precision describes the reliability of a predicted category. For example, if 100 passages receive an environmental-principle prediction and 90 carry the environmental reference label, precision is 90\%. Recall describes category coverage: if 100 passages carry the environmental reference label and the classifier identifies 80, recall is 80\%. F1 combines both requirements through the harmonic mean,
\begin{equation}
F1_k=\frac{2P_kR_k}{P_k+R_k},\qquad
\operatorname{MacroF1}=\frac{1}{9}\sum_{k=1}^{9}F1_k.
\label{eq:f1}
\end{equation}
Macro-F1 gives each principle equal weight, preventing the largest class from determining the entire summary. Support is the number of reference examples for a principle. Because the references are weak labels, ``correct'' in the numerical counts means agreement with the recorded assignment.

The principle and alignment intervals use 2,000 percentile bootstrap samples of CIN clusters, with seed 4206. A sampled company contributes all associated segments, preserving within-company dependence. Paired comparisons sample the same companies for both methods. Such intervals describe sampling variation within the available cohort; systematic label error and model-development exposure remain outside the interval. The 20-company score-validation intervals use 10,000 company-pair bootstrap samples with seed 42. Detector intervals were not calculated and are not inferred from the point estimates.

\subsection{Dataset accounting and source-specific results}
\label{sec:eval_sources}

The final single-label corpus contains 42,409 samples: 2,479 from explicit principle-axis facts, 36,513 from Section C mappings, and 3,417 from principle notes. The builder excludes 36,491 noisy facts and removes 2,640 groups carrying multiple labels. These counts describe different units: noisy fact rows and conflicting groups must not be added as though both were independent final examples.

The Section B funnel explains the small surviving source size. From 41,898 narrative candidates, 16,684 fail the content filter, leaving 25,214 clean candidates. Eighteen repeated same-label entries and 22,717 entries in conflicting groups are then excluded, leaving 2,479 rows. This is a reconciled current-data reduction. The historical count of 17,219 lacks a recoverable query and snapshot, so no historical expansion claim is made.

\subsection{Recovered classifier performance and training history}

The recovered seed-99 model matches 5,767 of 6,201 reference labels, giving Top-1 agreement of 93.00\% (95\% CI: 92.04--93.89\%). The reference appears in the two highest-ranked predictions for 6,057 examples, giving 97.68\% Top-2 agreement (95\% CI: 97.10--98.21\%). Macro-F1 is 0.9158 (95\% CI: 0.9036--0.9277). Fifty test texts exactly match a training text from a different company, accounting for 0.81\% of the test rows. Zero CIN overlap therefore does not imply complete text novelty.

Figure~\ref{fig:training} replaces the earlier four-epoch pilot curve with the saved five-epoch history for the final seed-99 run. The recorded validation macro-F1 rises from 0.9048 at epoch 1 to 0.9194 at epoch 4, then changes to 0.9189 at epoch 5. Validation loss rises over the same period. A loss penalises probability confidence as well as category choice, so an improving F1 and an increasing loss can coexist. The curve supports selecting epoch 4 by the registered category metric; the curve does not support a claim that overfitting is absent. No unrecorded sixth or additional adaptation epoch is plotted.

\subsection{Concept-disjoint and multi-label diagnostics}
\label{sec:concept_diagnostics}

Company separation tests transfer to another company, while concept separation asks whether a model can classify narratives from question concepts excluded from supervised training. The first diagnostic holds out 17 Section C concepts and evaluates 1,190 rows. On the same rows, the full-concept baseline reaches 97.98\% Top-1 agreement, while the concept-disjoint model reaches 87.31\%. The paired difference is $-10.67$ percentage points (95\% CI: $-12.36$ to $-8.96$). Both checkpoints underwent strict alias recovery before the reported re-evaluation.

The full-concept baseline uses seed 99, while the excluded-concept model uses seed 42 and fewer training examples. The paired interval describes the score difference on shared evaluation rows; the interval does not isolate concept exclusion from the other training differences. The result is consequently a diagnostic of transfer under a changed training condition.

Across three concept holdouts, Top-1 agreement averages 81.64\% with sample standard deviation 4.92 percentage points; Top-2 averages 95.34\% with standard deviation 1.31 percentage points. Macro-F1 averages 0.6752 with standard deviation 0.0170. Figure~\ref{fig:concept} shows the individual holdouts and associated example counts. The variation is across three holdout/training runs, not a confidence interval. The runs reuse the same company test population, and concept exclusion applies to supervised fitting rather than a verified concept-purged encoder corpus.

\input{figures/concept_holdouts}

An exploratory multi-label redesign groups company text and unions observed principle labels. The test population contains 5,934 grouped examples from the same 175 test companies. Hit@1 is 93.36\% (95\% CI: 92.54--94.11\%), micro-F1 at threshold 0.5 is 0.8898 (0.8743--0.9035), and macro-F1 is 0.8782 (0.8606--0.8942). The single-label baseline reaches Hit@1 of 93.55\% on the same grouped target. Multi-label set coverage improves under the changed output definition, while ranked-hit accuracy does not. Unioned labels can also combine generic text reused under many principles. This diagnostic is retained as an alternative target formulation rather than an across-the-board improvement.

\subsection{Frozen text-vagueness results}

The frozen language detector is evaluated on two distinct cohorts in Table~\ref{tab:detector}. Batches 1 and 2 contain 80 historical development examples. The detector flags 41 examples; 29 of the flags match the reference vagueness label. Of 37 reference-vague examples, 29 are found. These counts explain precision of 0.7073 and recall of 0.7838 in ordinary terms. Sixty of the 80 binary decisions match the reference, giving accuracy of 0.7500 and F1 of 0.7436.

Batch 3 contains 70 AI-annotated examples. The detector flags 35 examples, of which 21 match the AI vagueness label; 21 of 26 AI-labelled vague examples are identified. F1 is 0.6885. Figure~\ref{fig:detector} compares the three decision measures while keeping the two label sources separate. A difference between the cohorts cannot be attributed solely to model quality because both examples and annotation provenance differ.

\subsection{Supervised transfer versus zero-shot mapping}
\label{sec:eval_mapping}

Both mapping methods use the same 6,201 test examples. The MPNet definition-plus-anchor comparator reaches 67.63\% Top-1 agreement and 83.34\% Top-2 agreement. The recovered supervised classifier reaches 93.00\% and 97.68\%, respectively. Table~\ref{tab:mapping} reports the corresponding intervals. The paired Top-1 difference is $+25.37$ percentage points (95\% CI: $+24.02$ to $+26.71$). This is a comparison between supervised classification and zero-shot similarity ranking, with different access to labels. The result does not isolate the effect of domain adaptation and does not measure legal compliance.

\subsection{Historical human-rating comparison for the Transparency Score}

The retained human-rating cohort contains 20 companies. Pearson correlation is $r=0.6314$ (95\% CI: 0.2683--0.8519; $p=0.0028$), and Spearman rank correlation is $\rho=0.6285$ (95\% CI: 0.2321--0.8540). Mean absolute error is 12.16 points, root mean squared error is 15.19 points, and the audit reports $r^2=0.3986$. The squared correlation is an association summary and should not be read as a separately fitted predictive-regression result.

The rating file includes the automated score, so blindness to that score cannot be confirmed. Only one rater source is documented. The Zensar narrative packet had an extraction artifact involving URL-heavy content, and Geojit's business model highlights possible mismatch with manufacturing-oriented reporting indicators. Both companies remain in the headline cohort. Removing examples after inspecting residuals yields post-hoc sensitivity results rather than a replacement headline. Synthetic ratings, synthetic weight estimates, and AI assessments are not merged into the human cohort.
