\section{Results Across Four Reporting Levels}
\label{sec:results}

The preceding section evaluates analytical components. The following results instead show how saved outputs support review at four levels: one company, selected companies within one sector, the complete financial-sector group, and the full sector collection. This progression keeps a company-specific output separate from a validated model metric. All company scores below come from the saved score exports; no classifier retraining or new detector inference was performed for the manuscript revision.

The inspected database contains 1,164 company records and 1,172 filing records. The score export contains one row for each of 1,164 unique CINs, using the most recent available filing selected by the scoring script. These record counts describe the available snapshot and do not establish collection completeness against a national source index. The historical report of 1,164 out of 1,276 companies, or 91.2\%, is retained only as acquisition history, with no current audited source-coverage claim.

\subsection{One company: from filed facts to an interpretable outcome}
\label{sec:case}

Triveni Turbine Limited provides a worked example because the saved export includes all four score components and a sector benchmark. The corresponding database filing ends on 31 March 2026 and contains 1,862 raw facts. Table~\ref{tab:case_facts} shows three pairs of values stored with matching concept names, units, and periods. Scope 1 emissions rise, Scope 2 emissions fall, and water withdrawal falls. Different directions within one filing illustrate why a generic statement about ``environmental improvement'' cannot safely be matched to every environmental number.

The workflow begins by retaining those facts alongside narrative concepts and dimensions. Label construction can assign an eligible narrative to a principle using an axis tag, mapped concept, or principle note. The classifier can then rank the nine principles for an eligible passage. The language detector searches for evidence markers and vague phrasing, while numerical review routines can retrieve the comparative values shown in Table~\ref{tab:case_facts}. The saved case export does not contain a passage-level classifier prediction or a complete validated claim-verification outcome, so no new company-specific prediction is inferred from the overall model accuracy.

The scoring branch is fully decomposable. The filing contains 613 concepts accepted by the Completeness rule out of the 1,057-concept snapshot universe, giving $100(613/1057)=57.99\%$ after rounding. The scoring narrative selection yields 52 concept--text pairs. The saved Specificity of 63.46\% corresponds to 33 of 52 segments containing a recognised evidence pattern. The saved Clarity of 98.08\% corresponds to one flagged segment and 51 segments without a flag. The SDG component of 70.59\% corresponds to 12 of the 17 goals under the fixed correspondence. The segment counts are consistent with the saved percentages; the counts do not establish that every detected marker is externally substantiated.

Figure~\ref{fig:company_contributions} shows contributions rather than raw percentages, making the effect of each weight visible. Within the saved Machinery and Equipment group, the sector mean is 49.46 across 36 companies and the company's percentile is 94.4. The outcome indicates a comparatively high score under this implementation. The outcome does not establish comprehensive reporting, verified SDG delivery, or absence of misleading claims.

REC Limited has a saved composite of 67.56, compared with 29.83 for Vardhman Holdings. REC's Specificity is 57.69\%, whereas Vardhman's is 2.78\%; the corresponding Completeness values are 56.01\% and 35.00\%. The contrast identifies differences in detected disclosure content under one formula. Different financial business models, period availability, sector coding, and indicator applicability can also influence the comparison. A low score is a prompt to inspect missing or weakly evidenced content, rather than an allegation about business conduct.

Time comparison requires repeated filings for the same company. ESAF Small Finance Bank provides a financial-sector illustration: Completeness rises from 34.34\% to 54.59\%, Specificity from 30.23\% to 41.30\%, and Clarity from 93.02\% to 93.48\%, while SDG breadth remains 11.76\%. This decomposition explains the score arithmetic without treating a higher disclosure score as proof of improved sustainability performance.

\subsection{The complete financial-sector group}
\label{sec:all_finance}

Across all 94 saved financial-sector companies, the mean score is 48.17, the median is 49.79, and the standard deviation is 9.88. The range is 29.83--67.56, a spread of 37.73 points. Average Completeness is 49.77\%, Specificity 28.35\%, Clarity 90.67\%, and SDG breadth 16.27\%. High Clarity alongside much lower Specificity shows why an absence of vague phrases cannot substitute for concrete disclosure content.

The snapshot includes 77 companies whose latest recorded filing ends in March 2026 and 17 whose latest recorded filing ends in March 2025. The score CSV omits a filing identifier, so the year linkage is reconstructed from the current database and the latest-filing rule in the export script. This reconstruction is an explicit provenance limitation. The group is a latest-available snapshot, not a balanced annual panel. Apparent high-versus-low differences therefore cannot be interpreted as year effects.

The four bands in Table~\ref{tab:all_financial} show the distribution without a long company listing: 11 companies score at least 60, 35 score from 50 to below 60, 26 score from 40 to below 50, and 22 score below 40. The 94 observations do not provide an accuracy estimate because no independent 94-company human reference is available. The financial-sector mean is 0.56 points below the complete snapshot mean of 48.73; the difference is descriptive and small compared with within-sector variation.

\subsection{All sectors: coverage, score distribution, and interpretation}
\label{sec:all_sectors}

The final reporting level covers the 1,164 saved company scores across 72 recorded sector groups. Scores range from 26.88 to 75.58, with mean 48.73 and median 47.25. Forty-seven groups contain at least five companies and account for 1,109 scores. The remaining 25 groups contain 55 companies. Table~\ref{tab:all_sectors} lists all 47 comparison-eligible sectors and pools the 25 smaller groups in one clearly labelled row. Supplementary Table S2 retains every individual group, including the smaller sectors.

Among the 47 comparison-eligible groups, Warehousing and Transport Support has the highest saved mean, 54.55 across six companies. Wearing Apparel has the lowest mean, 42.33 across six companies, followed by Education at 42.43 across six companies. The difference between the highest and lowest means is 12.22 points. Both extreme groups are small, and no inferential sector-difference test or sector-mean confidence interval was calculated. The results therefore identify descriptive contrasts for inspection, not a verified ordering of sector sustainability quality.

Larger groups help place the extremes in context. Chemicals Manufacturing includes 101 companies with mean 49.56; Financial Services includes 94 with mean 48.17; Pharmaceuticals includes 65 with mean 51.18. Variation within such groups is substantial. For example, the Pharmaceuticals range is 32.38--73.72 and the Chemicals range is 32.18--75.34. Such ranges show that a sector mean alone cannot describe every company in the group. Sector coding and the common concept denominator also limit cross-sector fairness.

The earlier Pharmaceuticals example provides a second check on within-sector variation. Dabur India scores 73.72 and Kopran scores 32.38, a difference of 41.34 points within the same recorded group. Corresponding Completeness values are 73.13\% and 32.92\%, and Specificity values are 57.38\% and 23.68\%. Clarity exceeds 85\% for 58 of the 65 companies, so a high language-clarity value alone does not guarantee a high composite. Supplementary Table S4 preserves the earlier top-four and bottom-three company comparison with every component. This example describes variation under the saved sector assignments and does not establish the cause of the differences.

The four reporting levels lead to one practical outcome: an analyst can move from an individual component to a company score, from the company to a sector comparison, and from that sector to the full saved population. Every transition retains the denominator and the meaning of the output. No level converts a descriptive score into a verified greenwashing judgement.
