\begin{abstract}
India's Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR) framework produces a large collection of corporate disclosures that combine narrative claims with numerical information. Reviewing these reports requires analysts to locate supporting evidence and compare disclosures across companies. GRASP supports this review through a seven-module pipeline. Reports are collected and stored with the company name, reporting period, and measurement details. Principle labels are automatically assigned to selected text segments using report tags and concept mappings, creating a weakly labelled dataset. A BERT language model adapted to BRSR text is then trained to assign each text segment to one of nine reporting principles. Word lists and sentence similarity identify vague wording. Numerical and assurance checks create review records linked to the reported evidence. Classifier probabilities provide principle-level summaries, and a Transparency Score combines Completeness, Specificity, Clarity, and Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) breadth. The vagueness detector is reused to calculate Clarity. Company and sector comparisons are made available through an application programming interface. The application outputs cover 1,164 companies. On 6,201 text segments from 175 companies excluded from supervised training, the classifier achieves 93.00\% agreement with report-derived weak labels. The Transparency Score has a Pearson correlation of $r=0.6314$ with 20 historical human ratings. GRASP helps analysts find disclosures that need closer checking and inspect the evidence behind each result.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} BRSR; sustainability disclosure; weak supervision; BERT; vagueness detection; transparency scoring; sector benchmarking.
