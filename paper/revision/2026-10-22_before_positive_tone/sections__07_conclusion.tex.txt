\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Large sustainability-report collections are difficult to review manually. Finding positive or cautious wording is only one part of the task. An analyst also needs the relevant filed figure, reporting context, and a clear explanation of the company score. GRASP connects these steps through structured BRSR storage, filing-derived labels, principle classification, language checks, numerical review, and a four-part Transparency Score. Passages and figures remain linked to the same filing. Label sources remain visible during evaluation, and every score component has an explicit calculation. These features help direct review without treating a risk flag as proof of misconduct.

The recovered classifier agrees with weak labels on 93.00\% of test passages (95\% CI: 92.04--93.89\%) and reaches macro-F1 of 0.9158. The test set contains 6,201 passages from 175 companies excluded from supervised training. Results differ by source: 63.35\% for explicitly tagged narratives, 95.15\% for concept-mapped narratives, and 90.72\% for principle notes. Agreement also falls on concepts excluded from training. The text-vagueness detector reaches F1 of 0.7436 on 80 development examples; complete numerical-check accuracy remains unestablished. The score has Pearson correlation $r=0.6314$ with 20 historical human ratings (95\% CI: 0.2683--0.8519), with rating blinding unconfirmed. These results measure different parts of the workflow and retain separate limits.

The saved outputs cover 1,164 companies across 72 recorded sectors. Results move through four levels: one worked company, selected companies within a sector, the complete financial-sector group, and all sectors. Financial-company scores range from 29.83 to 67.56. Among the 47 sectors containing at least five companies, Wearing Apparel has the lowest mean, 42.33, and Warehousing and Transport Support has the highest, 54.55. For the eight companies with paired-year scores, the mean rises by 5.23 points, from 45.17 to 50.40. Six scores rise and two fall. Component changes explain the score movements, but this small group cannot establish a national trend or improved sustainability outcomes. Company, sector, and year views make the saved results easier to inspect while keeping score calculation separate from independent validation.

Future work should improve the evidence behind each output. Independent principle labels and broader blind human ratings would support stronger tests of the classifier, language detector, and score. Adaptation should use only declared training-company text, followed by testing on newly collected companies. Numerical checks need closer matches for measure, unit, period, business boundary, and valid zero values. Data collection could also include annexures and charts. Business descriptions could help correct sector labels and identify which reporting items apply. More annual filings would support repeated comparisons of the same companies under one scoring version, with filing identifiers recorded in every export. Extension to international sustainability frameworks would require clear mappings between reporting items and separate validation in each setting.
