\begin{abstract}
The Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR) brings sustainability information into a common format for India's listed companies. Reviewing these reports still requires analysts to connect written claims with filed figures and reporting periods. Existing word lists and text-similarity methods help find relevant passages, but cannot alone show whether a claim has matching numerical support. GRASP connects seven parts in one workflow. Structured digital filings (XBRL) are collected, and training labels are derived from filing tags and concept mappings. A BERT model adapted to BRSR text classifies passages under nine principles. Word lists and sentence similarity flag vague statements, while separate checks compare claims with filed figures. Principle mapping and a four-part Transparency Score support company and sector comparisons. An application programming interface makes the results available for review. The saved outputs cover 1,164 companies. On 6,201 test passages from companies excluded from supervised training, the classifier agrees with filing-derived labels in 93.00\% of cases. The Transparency Score has a moderate correlation with 20 historical human ratings ($r=0.6314$), although whether the rater saw the automated score is unconfirmed. GRASP helps analysts decide which disclosures need closer review. Independent labels and broader human assessment remain necessary to validate the language flags, numerical checks, and score.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} BRSR; sustainability disclosure; weak supervision; BERT; vagueness detection; transparency scoring; sector benchmarking.
