\section{Results Across Four Reporting Levels}
\label{sec:results}

The previous section tests the analysis methods. This section shows how saved outputs support review at four levels: one company, selected companies in one sector, the full financial-sector group, and all recorded sectors. A company-specific score is kept separate from a model-evaluation result. All company values below come from saved exports. No model was retrained and no new detector predictions were made for this manuscript revision.

The inspected database has 1,164 company records and 1,172 filing records. The score file contains one row for each of 1,164 unique CINs, based on the most recent available filing selected by the script. These counts describe the saved data. A comparison with a current national source index has not established collection completeness. The earlier figure of 1,164 out of 1,276 companies, or 91.2\%, is reported only as collection history.

\subsection{One company: from filed facts to an interpretable outcome}
\label{sec:case}

Triveni Turbine Limited is a worked example with all four score components and a sector comparison. The linked database filing ends on 31 March 2026 and contains 1,862 raw facts. Table~\ref{tab:case_facts} shows three previous/current value pairs with matching concepts, units, and periods. Scope 1 emissions rise, Scope 2 emissions fall, and water withdrawal falls. These different directions show why one general claim of environmental improvement cannot be compared with every environmental figure.

The workflow stores these figures beside narrative facts and reporting categories. An eligible passage receives a principle label from an explicit tag, concept map, or principle note. The classifier ranks the nine possible principles. The language detector looks for evidence markers and vague wording, while numerical checks can retrieve the value pairs in Table~\ref{tab:case_facts}. The saved case export does not contain passage-level classifier predictions or a fully validated claim-check result. Overall model accuracy therefore cannot be used to invent a prediction for this company.

Every part of the saved score can be explained. The filing has 613 accepted concepts out of 1,057 in the database, giving Completeness of $100(613/1057)=57.99\%$. Scoring retains 52 concept--text pairs. Specificity of 63.46\% corresponds to 33 of 52 passages with a recognised evidence pattern. Clarity of 98.08\% corresponds to one flagged passage and 51 without a flag. SDG breadth of 70.59\% corresponds to 12 of the 17 goals under the fixed map. These counts agree with the saved percentages. A matched marker, however, does not prove that the underlying claim has external support.

In the saved Machinery and Equipment group, 36 companies have a mean score of 49.46, and Triveni's percentile is 94.4. This is a relatively high score under the stated rules. The value does not establish complete reporting, verified SDG delivery, or the absence of misleading statements.

These differences identify where the saved disclosures differ under one formula. Business model, available reporting year, sector code, and applicable indicators can also affect the comparison. A low score directs attention to missing or weakly supported content. The value alone is not an allegation about company conduct.

A year comparison needs repeated filings for the same company. ESAF Small Finance Bank provides one example. Completeness rises from 34.34\% to 54.59\%, Specificity from 30.23\% to 41.30\%, and Clarity from 93.02\% to 93.48\%. SDG breadth stays at 11.76\%. The combined score rises from 42.59 to 54.10, or 11.51 points. Completeness adds about 8.10 points, Specificity 3.32 points, and Clarity 0.09 points to that change. This calculation explains the score increase without proving improved sustainability performance.

The column explains the arithmetic, not the underlying cause of the company's behaviour. Supplementary Table S3 retains both filings and every component. Filing identifiers and year labels are available, but the periods have not been independently audited. These eight companies do not establish a market-wide trend.

\subsection{The complete financial-sector group}
\label{sec:all_finance}

For all 94 financial-sector companies, the mean is 48.17, the median is 49.79, and the standard deviation is 9.88. Scores range from 29.83 to 67.56, a spread of 37.73 points. Mean Completeness is 49.77\%, Specificity 28.35\%, Clarity 90.67\%, and SDG breadth 16.27\%. Clarity is much higher than Specificity. This difference shows why avoiding vague phrases alone does not provide concrete evidence.

The latest recorded filing ends in March 2026 for 77 companies and March 2025 for 17. The original score CSV has no filing identifier. Year labels were therefore reconstructed from the database using the script's latest-filing rule. This link was not recorded in the original export. The group contains the latest available reports, rather than the same companies measured in one common year. Differences between high and low scores cannot therefore be read as year effects.

Table~\ref{tab:all_financial} groups the scores into four ranges. Eleven companies score at least 60, 35 score from 50 to below 60, 26 score from 40 to below 50, and 22 score below 40. Mean Specificity is 14.30\% in the lowest range and 47.24\% in the highest. The groups are formed from the combined score, which already uses Specificity. This pattern is descriptive and cannot independently validate the formula.


The full group shows how scores vary across financial companies, with a component breakdown available for every company. No independent human reference covers all 94 observations, so these scores do not provide an accuracy estimate. The financial mean is 0.56 points below the overall mean of 48.73. This difference is small beside the variation among companies in the same sector.

\subsection{All sectors: coverage, score distribution, and interpretation}
\label{sec:all_sectors}

The final level covers 1,164 company scores across 72 recorded sectors. Scores range from 26.88 to 75.58, with mean 48.73 and median 47.25. Forty-seven sectors contain at least five companies and account for 1,109 scores. The other 25 sectors contain 55 companies. Table~\ref{tab:all_sectors} lists the 47 larger groups separately and combines the smaller groups in one labelled row. Supplementary Table S2 lists all individual sectors.

Among the 47 sectors used for comparison, Warehousing and Transport Support has the highest mean, 54.55 across six companies. Wearing Apparel has the lowest mean, 42.33 across six companies. Education follows at 42.43, also across six companies. The highest and lowest means differ by 12.22 points. These groups are small, and no sector-difference test or confidence interval for the sector mean was calculated. The values describe the saved scores and do not establish a reliable ranking of sector sustainability quality.

The larger groups provide context. Chemicals Manufacturing has 101 companies with mean 49.56; Financial Services has 94 with mean 48.17; Pharmaceuticals has 65 with mean 51.18. Scores also vary widely within these groups. Pharmaceuticals spans 32.38--73.72, and Chemicals spans 32.18--75.34. A sector mean cannot describe every member company. Sector coding and the common concept list also affect the fairness of cross-sector comparisons.

The Pharmaceuticals example gives another view of company differences within one sector. Dabur India scores 73.72 and Kopran scores 32.38, a gap of 41.34 points. Completeness is 73.13\% and 32.92\%, and Specificity is 57.38\% and 23.68\%. Clarity exceeds 85\% for 58 of the 65 companies. High Clarity alone therefore does not guarantee a high combined score. Supplementary Table S4 preserves the four highest and three lowest examples with every component. These results use the saved sector labels and do not establish the cause of the differences.

The four levels let an analyst move from a component calculation to a company score, then to a sector comparison, and finally to the full company collection. At each step, the total used in the calculation and the meaning of the output remain clear. A descriptive score is not converted into a verified greenwashing finding.
