\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

India's Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting framework gives environmental, social, and governance disclosures a common structure. A 2021 circular from the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) made BRSR reporting mandatory for the top 1,000 listed companies by market capitalisation from financial year 2022--23. The framework covers nine principles of responsible business conduct. Essential indicators are mandatory, while leadership indicators are voluntary \cite{sebi2021}. A common format helps organise reports, but cannot establish whether every response is complete or correct.

A data analyst must complete several manual steps when reviewing a filing. First, a passage must be found and assigned to a topic. Next, a relevant numerical value must be located and checked against the reporting year, unit, and group covered. Comparing companies adds another challenge because business activities and applicable reporting items differ. Repeating these steps across hundreds of filings takes substantial time. For example, a statement that \emph{water use decreased} needs a matching water measure, business boundary, and comparison period before the claim can be assessed.

Automated text analysis can reduce this workload. ClimateBERT identifies climate-related language and selected disclosure categories. ChatReport retrieves report passages and answers reporting questions with source links. ESGReveal extracts structured information from narrative reports \cite{webersinke2021,ni2023,zou2025}. These methods help organise large report collections. Three questions remain when building a connected workflow for Indian structured filings.

The first question is \emph{how to connect a claim with evidence}. A phrase detector can find a promise, and a text-similarity model can identify an environmental topic. A supporting figure must still match the claim's meaning, period, and reporting context. The second question is \emph{whether the training labels are reliable}. Filing tags reduce manual labelling, but repeated question wording may help a model predict a tag without understanding the principle. The third question is \emph{what a company score means}. The calculation, included items, sector group, and validation source must be clear. Earlier research already covers parts of evidence retrieval and disclosure review. The contribution here is a connected BRSR workflow with clear tests and limits \cite{calamai2025,garg2025}.

GRASP addresses the first question by storing narrative passages and structured facts under the same filing. Language checks flag passages for review, while numerical and assurance checks keep the linked sentence and reported item available for inspection. The second question is addressed by recording three label sources and testing results separately by source, company, and excluded question concept. The third question is addressed by showing four score components and comparisons within recorded sectors. The contribution combines links to filed evidence, filing-derived training labels, detailed evaluation, and a score with a visible breakdown. A flag or score does not establish intent to mislead or legal compliance.

The study provides a 42,409-sample dataset with documented weak labels and separate companies for supervised training, validation, and testing. The classifier is assessed by label source and on concepts excluded from training. The language detector has a separate evaluation; the numerical checks remain review rules without a complete accuracy test. Company-score results then move from one worked example to a full financial-sector group and all recorded sectors. Each result answers a different question. Agreement with labels, agreement with human scores, saved company outputs, and a working service connection are reported separately.

The remaining sections of this paper follow the workflow from earlier research to practical use. Section~\ref{sec:literature} reviews related methods and Indian disclosure studies. Section~\ref{sec:methodology} explains the seven modules and score calculations. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} defines the test data and reports the audited model results. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the four reporting levels, from one company to all sector groups. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} explains the main limits and practical use. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarises the findings and future work.
